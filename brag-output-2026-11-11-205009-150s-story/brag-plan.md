## Narration

When disaster disrupts a community, the need for help can grow faster than people can find it. Someone needs a safe place to stay. Someone else has supplies, a vehicle, or time to give. But requests and offers can be scattered, and finding the right connection becomes another problem.

Community Help Network begins before that moment. It gives neighbors a shared place to ask for everyday help and offer what they can. A grocery run. A ride. A small task that makes a difficult day easier. Each connection helps a community practice showing up for one another.

Describe what you need, review the details, and create a request. Suggested connections explain why an offer might fit. Both participants choose whether to connect before private coordination opens. Then they can follow the request through and confirm the outcome. The point is a clear path from asking to helping, with people making the decisions.

Group activities, volunteers, and local organizations give that network more ways to grow. Ordinary participation creates a foundation that is already there when circumstances change.

When disaster strikes, the same familiar platform opens a dedicated response workspace. Bring urgent requests into view, organize the needs, and help coordinators focus their follow-up.

Missing-person notices and voluntary safe check-ins give the community a place to share updates. Adults’ names appear only with permission. Children’s details stay private. Community tips are shared privately with the reporter and coordinators. A check-in is self-reported, not a guarantee of safety.

Browse shelter and transport listings, see available resources and supplies, and use a geographic map for local context. These are tools for coordination, not live dispatch or a promise that help is available.

Coordinators can see unresolved requests, follow up, and track progress. Requester confirmation helps distinguish a connection from support that actually arrived.

Community Help Network connects ordinary kindness with practical disaster readiness. Build the relationships before the crisis. Keep the community connected when it matters most.
